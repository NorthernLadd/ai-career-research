# Growth evidence

## U.S. employment projections

BLS estimates how total employment in an occupation will change between 2025 and 2035. The last column gives our examples of related work. The percentages apply to the occupations in the first column across U.S. industries.

| BLS occupation | Projected employment change | Related work in this guide |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Data scientists | +34.6% | Predictive modeling and analysis |
| Computer and information research scientists | +21.8% | Experiments on model behavior and interpretability |
| Software developers | +10.2% | Building evaluation tools, inference services, and training-data pipelines |

A 34.6% increase corresponds to roughly 95,400 additional data scientist positions over the decade. It measures the projected difference between two employment totals. Annual openings also include jobs that become available when workers leave an occupation. [BLS occupational projections and worker characteristics](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)

## An observed signal from hiring

LinkedIn reported in September 2025 that U.S. hiring of AI engineering talent had risen **more than 25% year over year**. That is a historical measure of hiring activity observed on LinkedIn. It covers a group of workers and offers a different view from the BLS ten-year forecast of employment. [LinkedIn AI Labor Market Update, September 2025 (PDF)](https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/economicgraph/en-us/PDF/ai-labor-market-update-header-sept-2025.pdf)

## How to extend the research

To investigate whether a title is becoming more common, choose a geography and a fixed set of employers. Each month, save the date, exact title, location, posting URL, and requisition ID. Group title variants by duties and count a requisition once even if it appears in several cities. Track the share of all technical postings alongside the raw count so a hiring surge at one employer is visible.
